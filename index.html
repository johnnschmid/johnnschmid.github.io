Operating Principle

The extruder operates on the technology of direct transformation of PET bottles into a tape, and then into a filament suitable for 3D printing.This technology is called "pulltrusion", and consists of the fact that the PET bottle does not need to be crushed into crumbs used as raw material, but is simply cut into a tape, heated to 180 degrees, and pulled through a hotend-former using a winch. At such a low temperature, the plastic does not emit an odor, unlike the extrusion technology, which requires melting the material to a fluid state with all ensuing consequences. I wouldn't even call this process extrusion in full, rather — PET conditioning — preparing ready-made plastic for 3D printing.

Unlike industrial plants for plastic production, pulltrusion technology does not require expensive equipment (plastic grinder, pellet screw extruder, etc.) and consumes much less electricity. With this extruder, for example, from a single five-liter bottle you can get 16-19 meters of PET filament. The machine can be installed right on the 3D printer (or nearby) and print directly from the machine's feed spool. The machine operates from a 220V network, and there is also a 12V 4A version for work in, so to speak, "field conditions".

Transformation of Bottle into Tape

Before stripping the bottle to tape, last one should be prepaired. First, the bottom of the bottle is cut off and the label is removed. Next, the bottle is placed on the bottle cutter's guide. Then, the bottle passes through the bearing-equipped cutter via a special guide insert that limits the width of the strip being cut. At this stage, you can adjust the angle of the bottle (according to its diameter) and the width of the resulting tape using a changable preadjusted tape width guider.

⚙️

Tape speed: 4-5 mm/sec

Pulltrusion (PET conditioning)

Next, the tape enters the inlet funnel of the hotend-former, where heating and twisting of the tape into a tube takes place. The hotend-former is made from a prepared standard 3D printer nozzle (brass, stainless steel), a standard unmodified heat block, and a standard ceramic heating element. By the way, the width of the tape affects the filling percentage of the tube.

At the same time, the shape of the tube is more reminiscent of the letter "C" (c-type filament). The existing cavity in the filament is compensated by over-extrusion during 3D printing (I set from 130 to 150 percent in the slicer). The resulting filament is wound onto a spool using a reduction gearbox on a stepper motor.

📏

Ribbon width adjust: 6-7 mm

⚙️

Recommended extrusion flow in Slicer: 130% - 150%

Filament Welding

The machine is equipped with a filament welding device. The filament welder consists of two clothespins with teflon molds. To heat the filament in the welder, a NiCr wire coil with a cross-section of 0.2 mm is used, with a total thread length of 200 mm, operating from a built-in 12V power supply. The ends of the filament are installed in the clothespins, when the button is pressed, 12V is supplied to the coil, the ends of the filament are heated by the coil, melted, connected together, and formed by one of the clothespins (any) by pushing the welded pair inside the clothespin. The weld seam is cooled in teflon molds in 1-2 seconds. Thus, you can weld and accumulate filament on different spools, sorting filament by colors.

Printing Parameters

Printing with this filament is carried out at nozzle temperatures of 270–280°C and a bed temperature of 85°C — it is necessary to fine-tune the nozzle temperature based on the ease of pushing the filament through the extruder. A delicate part cooling fan is required during printing; otherwise, if cooling is insufficient, the plastic cools slowly and crystallizes. On larger models, I set the cooling fan within the 20–30% range. Several 3D printers were tested: a FlyingBear Ghost 5 Direct (teflon tube throat) and a Uni printer with a Titan Aero extruder (all metal throat, brass cube). No difference has been noticed so far. I use over-extrusion to compensate for the internal cavity within the filament (130–150%), as the filament is formed in the shape of a tube. An example of printing with this plastic is the machine itself.

Not every bottle can be recycled in supermarket

For example, 5L bottles don't fit into the recycling machine. During our experiments with various types of PET packaging, we discovered that containers with overly thick walls, non-standard geometry, or massive volumes exceed the physical limitations of standard intake slots and cutting mechanisms. Commercial recycling hardware relies heavily on automated recognition databases, meaning unbranded containers, custom-shaped bottles, or foreign imports featuring unfamiliar barcodes are frequently rejected by standard sorting systems. Additionally, containers with complex chemical coatings, heavy texturing, or stubborn glue residues require additional preparation steps. Careful selection of your raw plastic source is essential to ensure a smooth cutting process, consistent ribbon width, and overall high-quality filament production for your 3D printing needs.

Economics of Pulltrusion Technology

How much will a spool of filament obtained by drawing PET tape cost us? Let's calculate! One 5-liter water bottle yields 18–19 meters of plastic (c-type filament, 1.6–1.75 mm). A standard spool contains 300 meters of plastic. Thus, 1 spool requires 16 five-liter PET bottles. The average price of a 5-liter PET bottle on platforms like Avito is about 5 rubles. Total: 80 rubles for the raw materials needed to produce 1 spool of plastic. Electricity costs should also be taken into account, which are about 3 times less than 7.5 hours of 3D printer operation — exactly how long it takes to fill one spool with plastic.
